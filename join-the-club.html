Case study: Larisa Berg

The one who went all in
Mother of eight Ex coffee-shop owner Three-Star Ambassador

Larisa woke at 4am to bake the bread for her own shop. Today, the company pays her $20,000 a month — on the days she works and the days she doesn't.

Before Travorium, Larisa wasn't chasing a shortcut. She already owned the hard version of the dream — a little coffee shop and a bakery on the island of Hawaii, eight kids at home, a husband running his own construction company across the resorts.

Every morning started at four — sometimes three — to get the baking done. Loans. Payroll. Two years before the shop paid her a dime. And the trap she could never get out of:

“I open my doors, I do the sales, I close my doors. That means — no more sales.”

Then a friend called. Her husband nudged her — “let's just try it.” She opened the compensation plan, and one idea rewired how she thought about work for good.

Case study: Igor Nikitskiy

Larisa's husband · 5★
Licensed contractor Father of eight Five-Star Ambassador

His buddies laughed at his $10 commission checks. Then Igor ignored the account for a whole month, logged in — and found $3,200 waiting.

Igor wasn't looking for an opportunity — he didn't need one. He was a licensed general contractor in Hawaii with his own construction company, a wife running her café, and eight kids at home with a ninth on the way. So when his friend Tatiana kept calling, he let it ring.

“I never picked up the phone, because I knew she was going to sell me something.”

It was his wife who changed everything. She told him, “you're not a good man — pick up the phone, maybe she needs help.” He picked up.

And here's what makes it land: he didn't join for money. He'd once almost bought a $70,000 timeshare — $1,500 a year just to travel twice. Travorium was the opposite: a members' club into 2,000+ resorts for a fraction of that. With eight kids, cheap family travel is what sold him. He joined for the product. Then he found the comp plan.

His argument isn't “get rich.” It's simpler than that — and harder to wave off.
